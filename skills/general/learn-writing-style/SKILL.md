# Learn a Writing Style

Use this Official Skill to create or refresh a reusable, user-owned writing-style skill.

## Context, setup, and planning

Confirm whose voice to learn, what will be written, and the target channel, audience, and situation.
If the requested voice belongs to a team or brand rather than one person, say so explicitly and
keep it separate from any individual's voice. Offer flexible ways to gather examples:

- let the user select or paste writing they authored, such as sent messages, posts, proposals,
  reports, or decks;
- inspect approved authored work in Gmail, LinkedIn, or the app where the new writing will appear;
- for a team or brand voice, inspect approved brand guidelines, messaging documents, prior
  campaigns, macros, saved replies, or account and website copy;
- combine these approaches.

Prefer a small set of representative examples from the same channel and situation. For replies,
compare similar sent replies and read enough of each surrounding thread to understand why the
response took that shape. When the target spans several situations, distinguish variants such as
cold versus warm, short versus long, formal versus casual, and new message versus reply instead of
blending them into one generic style.

Do not treat received messages, copied templates, shared website copy, or another person's writing
as an individual's voice without confirmation. If examples are unavailable, agree on a simple
starting style and draft a couple of examples for the user to react to.

## Learn and preserve the style

1. Preserve a small set of approved concrete examples, not only labels such as “warm” or
   “professional.”
2. Note repeated patterns that affect output: openings, sentence length, formality, structure,
   punctuation, distinctive phrasing, calls to action, sign-offs, whether the writer apologizes or
   stays neutral, and language to avoid.
3. Separate stable voice from channel-, audience-, and situation-specific conventions. Record
   scoped variants when the writer changes register rather than averaging those differences away.
4. Show a concise summary and a representative draft for review. Incorporate the user's
   corrections, and repeat the small-sample check before scaling to a batch or an unfamiliar
   channel.
5. Save only the approved examples and minimum useful observations in the user-owned writing-style
   skill. Update it as the user corrects future drafts.

Keep calibration drafts in review. This skill does not authorize sending or publishing. If the
larger workflow includes an external action, follow the applicable Official Skill for recipient
verification, channel constraints, and approval.

## Suggested outcome

Create a calibrated writing-style skill grounded in real, approved examples and ready to guide
writing in the target situations without flattening distinct voices or registers.
